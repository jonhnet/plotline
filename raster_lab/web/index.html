These are starting points for experiments. Centerlines use pixel darkness and thinning; edges use Canny detection. Neither understands subjects or recovers an artist’s intent. Fixed-width centerlines currently lose variation in stroke weight.

Hatching uses a simple ordered screen. Percentages scale requested ink coverage; they are not measured paper coverage. CMY/CMYK use illustrative ink colors and an approximate overlap model. Displayed coverage and blurred RGB error describe the preview, not calibrated physical ink.

Distances come from the downloadable vector paths. “Paths” counts separate pen-down runs before travel optimization. Color error averages RGB differences after a 1 mm blur; lower means closer broad colors, not necessarily better art. These metrics are separate from PDF decoding tests.

Original downloaded bytes are preserved. Color screening uses at most 1,000 pixels on the longest edge; fine-detail recovery and lettering use native pixels up to 2,048 without upscaling. Previews model nominal pen width at 150 dpi. Wave experiments vary amplitude, frequency, or both; their near-white cutoff lifts the pen to keep highlights clear. A carrier tone remains in other light areas. Source images retain artist credits and are used here as private development challenges.

Edge emphasis: significant hue or brightness changes attract CMY ink on both sides, retaining the source color on each side. Demand fades to white over 0.25″ or 0.5″; light regions progressively omit rows so the straight wave carrier can disappear. Recovered ink follows narrow dark valleys with lighter surroundings on both sides, avoiding the paired outlines produced by Canny. It can still miss weak strokes, junctions and broad brush marks; stroke weight remains fixed. The new filter includes the earlier CMY and hybrid controls.
